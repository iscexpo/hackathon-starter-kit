# Mentor Guide

Mentors help teams learn and make better decisions; they do not take over a team's project or write a submission on its behalf.

## A useful mentoring conversation

1. Ask the team to explain the user, problem, and desired outcome.
2. Help them reduce scope to a testable end-to-end flow.
3. Ask what they have already tried before proposing fixes.
4. Explain trade-offs, risks, and how they can verify a solution.
5. Encourage inclusive design, accessibility, privacy, and clear attribution.
6. Leave implementation decisions and final work to the team.

## Fairness and boundaries

- Apply the same level of support fairly across teams.
- Disclose judging, employment, financial, family, or other conflicts to organizers.
- Do not share another team's private idea or repository.
- Do not ask teams to provide credentials or personal data.
- Follow the event's rules on AI tools, third-party assistance, and authorship.
- Escalate safety or conduct concerns through the event's private reporting route.

## Suggested prompts

- What is the smallest demo that would prove your idea works?
- How will you know the intended user can complete the main task?
- What could fail during a live demo, and what is your backup?
- What assumptions have not yet been tested?
- What does the project intentionally not do?
