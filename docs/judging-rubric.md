# Judging Rubric

This is a suggested 100-point rubric. The organizer must publish the final rubric and any track-specific changes before the event begins. Judges should score the work that is demonstrated and documented, not just the pitch or team resources.

## Scoring table

| Criterion | Points | What judges look for |
|---|---:|---|
| Problem understanding and relevance | 20 | Clear problem, defined users, evidence or reasoning for why it matters, appropriate scope |
| Functionality and execution | 25 | Core user journey works; implementation fits the stated goal; demo and tests support the claims |
| Creativity and technical approach | 15 | Thoughtful idea, appropriate use of technology, good technical decisions, useful iteration |
| Usability and accessibility | 15 | Easy-to-follow flow, clear language, accessible interaction and readable presentation |
| Impact, inclusion, and responsibility | 15 | Plausible benefit, attention to different users, privacy/security awareness, responsible data and AI use |
| Demo and communication | 10 | Clear demonstration, honest description of completed vs. planned features, coherent explanation |
| **Total** | **100** | |

## Rating scale

Score each criterion using this scale, then convert the rating to the criterion's point value (rating ÷ 5 × criterion maximum).

| Rating | Guidance |
|---:|---|
| 1 — Limited | Criterion is mostly unaddressed or cannot be verified from the submission. |
| 2 — Early | Some evidence is present, but major gaps limit confidence or usefulness. |
| 3 — Solid | Meets the expected standard for a time-limited prototype. |
| 4 — Strong | Thoughtful, well-executed work with minor gaps. |
| 5 — Exceptional | Clear, compelling evidence of quality, fit, and care for this criterion. |

Judges should add short evidence-based notes for each criterion. A prototype does not need to be production-ready to score well; teams should be evaluated against the published scope and the time available.

## Fair judging practices

- Calibrate the rubric together before scoring. Discuss sample interpretations without preselecting winners.
- Disclose conflicts of interest and recuse from affected entries.
- Apply the same criteria to all teams in a track.
- Do not award points solely for expensive tools, paid APIs, polished equipment, or prior access to resources.
- Give accessible explanations and consider reasonable demo-format accommodations.
- Distinguish evidence from assumptions; ask the same core questions of comparable teams.
- Keep scores and comments factual, respectful, and limited to event-authorized systems.

## Suggested tie-break order

Publish the tie-break order before submissions close. Suggested order: (1) Functionality and execution, (2) Problem understanding and relevance, (3) Impact, inclusion, and responsibility, (4) a short standardized judge-panel discussion using written evidence. Do not create new criteria after seeing the finalist list.
